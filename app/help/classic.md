title: Classical segmentation
figure: Denoise → enhance → background → threshold → refine → clean → instances

The conventional recipe, no model needed: the image is denoised, the feature of interest is enhanced, the background is flattened with a white top-hat, a threshold turns it into a mask, an active contour moves that mask onto the object's own boundary, the mask is cleaned, touching objects are split by a watershed, and the objects that are not what was being looked for are filtered out by shape. Every stage is a parameter and every stage can be left off, so the step can be tuned in seconds and it runs on any machine.

$$
M = \left[\, G_\sigma * \big(I - (I \circ B_r)\big) > \tau \,\right],\qquad L = \text{watershed}\big(-d(M)\big)
$$

## Presets

Almost every setting below follows from what is being segmented, so the step offers a starting point per structure. Choosing one writes its values into the fields — an ordinary undoable change — and every field stays editable afterwards; a preset is not a mode, and the step remembers values, not which preset they came from.

| Preset | For |
|---|---|
| **Nuclei** | Round, roughly convex objects that touch. Otsu, opening, hole filling, and a distance watershed on peaks that stand clear of their surroundings. |
| **Cells (touching)** | Objects pressed flat against each other, where there is a visible boundary but no waist. Median denoise, a rolling-ball background, and the gradient watershed. |
| **Puncta** | Small round spots of more than one size. A difference of Gaussians, then blob centres over a radius range, which finds objects of different sizes without retuning. |
| **Filaments** | Long thin structures traced whole. Frangi tubes over three widths, connected components, and hysteresis so a filament that fades stays one object. |
| **Filament network** | A mesh with crossings. Meijering, which answers at a junction where Frangi treats it as blob-like and cuts the network apart. |
| **Centrelines** | The network again, thinned to the line down the middle of each filament: what a length is measured on, and a useful training label. |
| **Faint or noisy** | Weak signal on a busy background. Anisotropic diffusion, a rolling-ball background, the Triangle cut, hysteresis and an active contour to finish the boundary. |

On the bundled pipeline the filament presets return 54 objects (Filaments) and 23 (Filament network); the object presets are for other data and return one or two objects there, which is what a preset chosen for the wrong structure looks like.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Denoise** <br> none · median · diffusion | Run before everything else, on the raw values. A 3×3 *median* removes shot noise without moving an edge, which a Gaussian cannot do. *Anisotropic diffusion* (Perona–Malik) smooths the inside of a region and leaves its boundary in place: the conductance falls off as the local gradient rises, so an edge steeper than *Diffusion edge* survives every step. Use it when the noise is bad enough that a threshold speckles but a blur would close the gaps between objects. |
| **Diffusion steps · edge** | How many diffusion steps, and the gradient that counts as an edge, as a fraction of the image's intensity range. More steps flatten more; a smaller edge keeps more detail. |
| **Enhance** <br> none · blobs · tubes · neurites | What to bring out before the threshold. *Blobs* is a difference of Gaussians, a band-pass that answers to round objects about *Feature σ* across and flattens anything broader, so nuclei survive a textured or uneven background. *Tubes* is Frangi vesselness in 3D: the Hessian's eigenvalues at several widths score how tube-like each voxel is, so a filament is found whatever direction it runs in, including through z. It keeps filaments continuous where an intensity cut breaks them into dashes. *Neurites* is Meijering's filter, which mixes the same eigenvalues (l = (1−a)·e + a·tr H) before taking the largest instead of demanding the tube-like pattern of one large and two small. The difference is at a crossing: Frangi treats a junction as blob-like and suppresses it — on a synthetic crossing its response there falls to 0.22 of the shaft, which cuts the network into a separate object per segment — while Meijering's rises to 1.55. Frangi gives the cleaner filament-to-background contrast; Meijering keeps a network in one piece. |
| **Feature σ · σ max · Scales** <br> px | Blobs: the radius to respond to. Tubes: the smallest and largest width, and how many widths in between; the score is the best over the range. |
| **Background** <br> top-hat · rolling ball | How the background is estimated. The *top-hat* opens the image with a box, which follows a flat background and no more. The *rolling ball* rolls a ball of the same radius along the underside of the intensity surface and takes the surface its top traces — a grey opening with a hemispherical structuring element — so it follows a background that curves, which is what uneven illumination or a thick specimen actually gives. It matches scikit-image's `rolling_ball` on the cases tested. It is run on a decimated copy and interpolated back, as ImageJ does, because a full-resolution ball of radius *r* costs (2r+1)² per pixel. Note the order: the background is removed *after* the enhancement, so with Tubes or Neurites on it is subtracted from the ridge response and not from the raw image — on the bundled SIM reconstruction a ball of 12 after Neurites cuts the coverage from 14.3 % to 1.5 %. Leave the radius at 0 when an enhancement is already doing the flattening. |
| **Background radius** <br> px | Structures larger than this radius are background and are removed. Use about the object size; 0 leaves the background alone. |
| **Smoothing σ** <br> px | Gaussian blur before the threshold; suppresses noise that would otherwise fragment the mask. |
| **Threshold** <br> Otsu · Triangle · Li · Yen · Isodata · Multi-Otsu · Manual · Percentile · Local mean · Local contrast | Otsu picks the cut that best separates two intensity classes, which assumes the two are of comparable weight; in a mostly empty field they are not, and it cuts into the signal. *Triangle* instead puts the cut at the foot of the background peak, the corner of the histogram, so it keeps every dim object and lets some background in. *Li* minimises the cross entropy between the two classes and lands between the two. *Yen* maximises an entropic correlation criterion and settles at the top of the background mode, which suits a faint signal. *Isodata* (Ridler–Calvard) iterates until the cut is halfway between the two class means, so unlike Otsu it does not care how much of the image each class occupies — the choice when the objects are a small part of the field but clearly separated from it. *Multi-Otsu* fits three classes and keeps only the brightest, which drops a mid-grey halo the single cut swallows. Percentile keeps the brightest fraction; Manual is a fixed value in the (smoothed) intensity units. *Local mean* compares each pixel with the mean of its window. *Local contrast* puts the cut *k* local standard deviations above that mean, so it follows the background level and the local noise together and does not turn a flat region into speckle. |
| **Local window · ratio · offset · contrast k** <br> local methods | Window side in pixels. Local mean: foreground where value > ratio × mean + offset. Local contrast: where value > mean + k × SD + offset. |
| **Hysteresis · low** <br> on · off | Two cuts instead of one: everything above the threshold is kept, and so is everything connected to it down to the lower cut, which sits *Hysteresis low* of the way from the image floor up to the threshold. A filament that fades along its length stays one object instead of breaking into dashes, and an isolated speck that never reaches the upper cut is still discarded. |
| **Refine** <br> none · active contour | Morphological Chan–Vese: the mask boundary is moved, step by step, to the two-region fit of the image — the inside mean against the outside mean — and smoothed by a morphological curvature operator. There is no edge term and no shape assumption, so it works on filaments as readily as on cells. It repairs a mask that leaked into the background or pinched off part of an object; give it a mask that is roughly right and it finds the boundary the threshold could not. It fits the same image the threshold saw, so with an enhancement on it fits the enhanced response, not the raw intensity. |
| **Refine steps · smoothing** | How many contour steps, and how many curvature rounds inside each. More smoothing gives a rounder contour and removes thin arms; 0 leaves the region force to act alone. |
| **Opening radius** <br> px | Binary opening (erode then dilate) drops specks and thin bridges narrower than the radius. |
| **Fill holes** <br> on · off | Background enclosed by an object, per plane, becomes object: hollow nuclei stay one object. |
| **Fill holes (3D)** <br> on · off | The same in three dimensions. A cavity inside a solid object is enclosed only by way of the planes above and below it, so the per-plane fill cannot see it — which is most cavities in a stack. Cavities are filled one at a time, so **Largest hole** can leave a real lumen open while closing the small ones. |
| **Instances** <br> watershed (distance) · watershed (gradient) · components | The *distance* watershed splits touching objects at the waist between them, which needs a waist to exist. The *gradient* watershed floods the image's own edges instead, so it separates two objects that press flat against each other as long as there is a visible boundary between them. Connected components keeps every connected blob as one object. |
| **Seeds** <br> distance maxima · h-maxima · blob centres | What the watershed starts from. *Distance maxima* takes every peak of the distance map at least *Seed distance* apart, so a lumpy or elongated object is often cut into pieces. *H-maxima* keeps only the peaks that stand *Seed depth* above their surroundings, so one object stays one object. Raise the depth when an object is split, lower it when two are merged. *Blob centres* instead finds the objects in the image itself: the scale-normalised Laplacian of Gaussian is evaluated over a range of sizes and peaks once at the centre of each round object, whatever its size. That is the one to use when the objects are not all the same size, which is where a distance map has to be retuned for each. |
| **Object radius · max · scales** <br> px, blob centres | The smallest and largest object radius to look for, and how many sizes in between. The detector answers to every size in the range at once. |
| **Min. voxels · Max. voxels** | Objects outside the range are dropped. The maximum is the usual way to remove a clump that the splitting failed on. |
| **Min. fill** | Drop objects filling less than this fraction of their bounding box. Scattered debris fills very little of its box; a cell fills most of it. |
| **Max. elongation** | Drop objects whose bounding box is longer than this many times its width, measured in the plane. |
| **Expand labels** <br> px | After everything else, grow every object outwards into the background by this much, nearest object first. It closes the gap an opening or a watershed line left without changing which object a voxel belongs to: a voxel the same distance from two objects stays background, so two objects never fuse. Meant for compact objects — on a filament network even 2 px more than triples the coverage. |
| **Centrelines** <br> on · off | Replace every object with the line down its middle: one voxel thick, the same length, the same topology, the object's own id kept. Topological thinning, deleting only voxels whose removal changes neither the object's connectivity nor the background's, and keeping end points so a curve thins to a line instead of eroding away. It is what a filament's length is measured on, and a centreline is a useful training label in its own right. Thinning can leave a short spur where a corner forces one. Everything else — the review table, the shape filters, the box measurements — is computed before this, on the objects as they were segmented. |
| **Drop border objects** <br> on · off | Objects touching the x / y edge are cut off by the field, so neither their size nor their shape can be measured. Leave it on when the measurements matter, off when the masks are training data and a partial object is still a useful example. |

## Note

Start with Otsu, σ 1, opening 1 and the watershed on h-maxima seeds. When the objects vary in size, switch the seeds to blob centres and give their radius range.

For filaments rather than compact objects the recipe is different, and these defaults are wrong for it: turn on *Tubes*, set the widths to the filament thickness (σ 0.8 – 2.0 over three scales suits the bundled SIM reconstruction), leave the smoothing and the opening off, and take *Connected components* — a distance watershed has nothing to split there. Add *Hysteresis* there too: it is what keeps a fading filament in one piece. Measured on the bundled pipeline (`examples/sim_bundled.sirius.toml` with a Classical step appended, so the input is the volume reconstruction): that recipe with a plain Otsu cut returns 58 fragments covering 2.8 % of the field; with hysteresis at 0.5, 54 longer filaments covering 5.1 %; with *Triangle* in place of Otsu, 12 covering 10.5 %. Swapping *Tubes* for *Neurites* returns 23 objects at 14.6 %, because the junctions Frangi cuts stay connected. Lower cuts join more, so check that the network has not become one object. A plain intensity threshold on that data returns one object: the whole cell. When the illumination is uneven, switch the threshold to Local contrast or add a top-hat of about the object radius. When objects sit in texture, turn on the blob enhancement at their radius. When one object keeps being split, raise the seed depth. When two objects touch flat, with a boundary but no waist, switch the instances to the gradient watershed. When the mask is nearly right but its edge is not, turn on the active contour. When the labels are training data and precision matters more than recall, add the shape filters. For crowded or low-contrast data a learned model (the Segmentation step with Cellpose) still separates objects the classical route cannot.
